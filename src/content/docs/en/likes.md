> [!note] Still in pilot
> Likes are currently switched on for a small group of publications. They'll be available to everyone soon. Want to try them already? Let [me](mailto:ernst-jan@lettership.com) know.

> [!tip] In short
> - Under every edition you ask readers **explicitly** for a like. That works far better than a button tucked away in a footer.
> - After a like, Lettership asks your reader to **pass your article on personally** to someone who would appreciate it too.
> - Under your article on your site you can see how often it was liked.

## Why we built likes this way

From conversations with writers I know how important it is that you **feel read right away**. Moving to Lettership is nerve-racking enough. Everyone asks me: won't this hurt my growth? If you then hit send and hear nothing but crickets, that's a risk. I want you to get so much more appreciation via Lettership than via Substack that you can't help telling other writers about it.

Substack no longer treats the Like button as important: it sits hidden in the generic footer. In my own newsletter I noticed it can be different. At the end of 2025 I asked readers explicitly for a like for the first time: "Did you find this newsletter worthwhile? Let me know with a heart below. I'll head into Christmas extra cheerful if I can see who's happily reading along." **Suddenly people started clicking en masse.** It felt a bit like begging, but it worked remarkably well. I believe people genuinely enjoy letting you know.

That's why on Lettership the invitation sits in the email itself, in words you write yourself.

> [!example] It works
> A writer who moved over from Substack noticed that likes on Lettership are about **three times as high** as on Substack. My explanation: there's a clear call to action in the email, and we present it as "let the author know", whereas on Substack it's just a button in the footer. That writer also finds it "really encouraging".

### Why not just "reply to me"?

You can, of course, and replies to your newsletter arrive in your inbox (see [Profile](../instellingen/profiel/)). But many writers find that hard: every email feels like an obligation to write back, with the guilt that comes when you don't. A like asks nothing in return.

### Why so simple?

This is deliberately the smallest version that works. Readers don't have a Lettership account yet, so under your article there's only a counter, without names or profile pictures, and readers can't like on the page itself yet. If it works, we'll build it out.

### Why pass it on instead of sharing on social media?

Someone who just gave a like is enthusiastic about your piece. That's the best moment to ask whether they know someone who would appreciate it too. **Personally, to one person**, via WhatsApp, Signal or email, not through a generic share bar for X or LinkedIn. A recommendation from a friend works better than a post in a timeline.

## How it works for your readers

1. **At the bottom of your edition** is your invitation with a like button.
2. **After a click** your reader lands on a thank-you page, saying we'll let you know.
3. **On that page** we ask whether your reader knows someone who would appreciate your article too, with buttons to pass it on:
   - **WhatsApp**
   - **Signal**: the link is copied, so your reader can paste it in Signal
   - **Email**
   - **Copy link**
   - **More…** (phone only): the phone's own share menu
4. **Under your article** on your site you can see how often it was liked.

We only send the title and the link, no text on your reader's behalf.

## How to switch it on

Go to **Settings › Mail**. In the **Likes** block, switch on asking readers for likes and write the **invitation** that appears at the bottom of every edition. A default text with your name is already there.

> [!tip] Write it in the first person
> Make the invitation personal and say honestly why you'd love it. It may feel like begging, but people are happy to let you know.
